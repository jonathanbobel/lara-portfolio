## Overview

As Fathom transitioned into IQVIA, HCIC 2026 marked an important milestone: the first time the two teams would show up together as one unified IQVIA presence at a major healthcare event.

The event was an opportunity to introduce a clearer, more cohesive story about our combined capabilities, engage prospective clients, and strengthen existing relationships.

The goal was to carry that story consistently across every event touchpoint, from the physical booth to supporting presentations, print collateral, and digital content.

## The Challenge

This wasn’t simply a matter of updating signage or replacing one brand with another. It was about bringing two teams together under a shared identity and making that transition feel cohesive to our audience.

The experience needed to communicate a clear, growth-oriented story across a broad portfolio of solutions and capabilities, while working within IQVIA’s established brand standards.

With multiple stakeholders, an external booth builder, and a range of deliverables, the challenge was to create a consistent experience that felt like one brand rather than a collection of individual materials.

## My Role

I led the creative direction for the event, collaborating with team members and stakeholders on messaging, design concepts, and layout. Working alongside a third-party booth builder, I helped translate our direction into a cohesive physical experience.

I also designed and developed the supporting presentations, print collateral, and digital content, adapting my creative approach to the IQVIA brand while maintaining consistency across every touchpoint.

## The Approach

### 1. Establishing a unified story

We worked collaboratively to align the messaging and visual direction around a common client challenge: growth.

Rather than presenting individual solutions in isolation, the goal was to communicate how IQVIA’s data, marketing solutions, and agency services could work together to support health system growth.

### 2. Translating the brand into an experience

The booth became the central expression of that unified presence.

I worked through the visual application, messaging, and layout in coordination with the external booth builder, balancing the requirements of the physical space with IQVIA’s brand system.

Clear, benefit-oriented messaging and recognizable solution pillars helped communicate the breadth of our capabilities without overwhelming the audience.

### 3. Extending the experience across touchpoints

The booth was only one part of the event. I developed supporting presentations, print collateral, and digital assets that carried the same visual language and messaging throughout the experience.

Rather than treating each deliverable as a separate piece, the work was developed as one connected brand experience.

## The Design Solution

The final experience brought the IQVIA identity forward through a strong, recognizable brand presence, supported by clear messaging, consistent visual language, and content focused on our combined solutions and capabilities.

From the booth environment to the supporting materials, the creative direction emphasized simplicity, consistency, and accessibility, helping make a complex portfolio easier to understand and navigate.

## Impact

HCIC 2026 was an important step in presenting Fathom and IQVIA as one unified team.

The post-event debrief reinforced the value of the approach. The team described the growth-oriented story as clearer, simpler, and more direct than in the past, with the combined portfolio creating more natural opportunities to engage clients and prospects around their broader business challenges.

The strong IQVIA presence, straightforward booth messaging, clear solution pillars, and concise collateral were all identified as elements worth carrying forward into future events.

Beyond the individual deliverables, the project helped make the brand transition visible and tangible to the market, establishing a foundation for how the combined team could continue showing up together.

## Reflection

> A unified brand presence doesn’t happen simply because everyone is using the same logo.

It takes alignment around the story, thoughtful application of the visual identity, and attention to how every interaction contributes to the larger experience.

HCIC reinforced the value of thinking beyond individual assets and approaching an event as an integrated brand experience. It also reminded me that the experience doesn’t end when the design is complete.

The post-event feedback identified opportunities to improve how the booth supported conversations, how attendees interacted with content, and how materials could better connect to follow-up discussions.

Those insights are an important part of the creative process. A successful experience isn’t necessarily a finished one. There’s always an opportunity to learn from how people interact with the work and make the next experience better.
