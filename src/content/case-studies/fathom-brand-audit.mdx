## Overview

As Fathom evolved, its visual brand had become increasingly inconsistent across departments and communications. The challenge wasn’t a lack of appreciation for the brand; it was a lack of accessible tools, clear standards, and shared understanding of how to apply it.

As Fathom’s sole creative team member, I led a comprehensive brand audit to understand how the brand was being used, identify barriers to consistency, and establish a more practical framework for its application.

The work resulted in updated design standards, a refreshed PowerPoint template, and supporting Word and marketing layout standards—giving employees the tools and guidance to communicate with greater consistency and confidence.

## The Challenge

Fathom’s brand was being applied across the organization by people who relied on everyday tools like PowerPoint and Word to create and share materials.

While established design systems can provide sophisticated creative direction and flexibility at the designer level, those systems don’t always translate easily into the tools employees use every day to communicate with clients and prospects.

The result was a disconnect between how the brand was intended to look and how it was being applied in practice. Employees were often working under tight deadlines, reusing outdated documents, or making their own design decisions within tools that offered limited creative flexibility.

The opportunity I saw was to bridge that gap and translate the brand into a practical, accessible system that made consistency achievable for everyone, regardless of design experience or software proficiency.

## The Approach

I led a four-part discovery and development process, beginning with an understanding of how employees were actually using the brand.

1. **Discovery:** Audited existing brand deliverables across departments, organizing shared assets and representative materials to understand the current state.
2. **Stakeholder research:** Interviewed employees across the organization to understand their workflows, frequently used materials, pain points, and opportunities for improvement. Their perspectives helped identify where the brand system wasn’t meeting everyday needs.
3. **Findings and recommendations:** Synthesized the research into actionable priorities, separating immediate improvements from longer-term brand opportunities.
4. **Design and implementation:** Developed updated design standards and templates, presented recommendations to leadership, and guided the rollout across the organization.

## Key Findings

The research revealed three primary opportunities:

1. **Reduce friction:** Tight deadlines and hard-to-find templates encouraged employees to reuse outdated documents rather than start with approved materials.
2. **Reconnect employees with the brand:** The brand had evolved, but internal communication and education hadn’t kept pace.
3. **Prioritize everyday touchpoints:** PowerPoint and Word were the most frequently used brand applications and offered the greatest opportunity for immediate improvement.

> Importantly, every employee interviewed complimented Fathom’s brand and professional presence. The goal wasn’t to reinvent the identity, but to make it work better.

## The Design Solution

I translated the audit findings into a more accessible design framework, focusing on the everyday tools employees relied on most: PowerPoint and Word.

Rather than expecting non-designers to interpret broad brand guidelines and make complex design decisions, I developed practical standards and templates that built consistency into the tools themselves.

The approach focused on simplifying choices, establishing clear visual hierarchies, and providing repeatable layouts and guidance that worked within the limitations of these applications without sacrificing the brand’s identity.

The goal wasn’t to eliminate flexibility or creativity. It was to make the right design decisions easier and more intuitive, giving employees the confidence to create professional, on-brand communications without needing a designer to oversee every document.

The result was a more usable extension of the Fathom brand, translating the intended creative direction into everyday execution.

## Leadership and Rollout

I presented the audit findings and recommendations to leadership, secured approval for the proposed direction, and moved the work into implementation.

Following approval, I introduced the updated standards and templates company-wide through an all-hands presentation. This helped communicate the changes, explain their purpose, and reinforce the importance of consistent brand application.

## Impact

The project established a more cohesive and accessible framework for Fathom’s everyday communications.

More importantly, the updated templates and standards became part of the organization’s ongoing workflows. They remained in use until the Fathom brand was sunset following the transition to IQVIA in Q4 2026, demonstrating the lasting value of a system designed around employees’ everyday needs.

The result was more than a design refresh. It was a practical brand system built around the people who needed to use it every day.

## Reflection

At the time, the challenge was to make the Fathom brand easier to use within the tools people already knew: PowerPoint and Word. Meeting people where they worked was an important part of making the system accessible.

But would those tools have been the best long-term solution?

With the opportunity to approach this again, I’d explore whether a more purpose-built platform could offer a better balance between creative flexibility and brand consistency. Could a more connected system make the right design choices easier while providing guardrails that reduce inconsistency?

I’d also want to understand the trade-offs: the learning curve, adoption, accessibility, cost, and whether introducing a new tool would actually improve the experience or simply create another barrier.

I don’t see this as a flaw in the original solution. I see it as the next question I’d bring to the table.
